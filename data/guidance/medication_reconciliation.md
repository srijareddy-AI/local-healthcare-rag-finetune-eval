# Medication Reconciliation at Discharge — Public Educational Summary

This is a short educational summary written for this demo project. It is NOT a
clinical guideline and NOT medical advice for any real patient.

Medication reconciliation means building one accurate, complete list of the
medications a patient should take after discharge, and comparing it with the
lists from before admission and during the hospital stay. Common sources of
error are duplicated medications, omitted medications, wrong doses, and
instructions that are unclear about timing. A good discharge list names each
medication, its dose, and how often it is taken, and states what to do if a
dose is missed only when the treating clinician has given that instruction.
If any part of the list is missing or unclear, the safe action is to flag it
as missing and ask the care team, not to guess or fill it in.
